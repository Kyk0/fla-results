The two teachers

Before a camera can learn from the sole, the sole's labels have to be good. Two step-level classifiers act as teachers: one reads only the sole, one reads only joints and IMU. Here is how well each names the floor, on gaits it was trained on, on a gait it never saw, and under other sensor models.

Simulation only. Booster K1 in MuJoCo, simulated sole. Six floor classes, three gaits.

In short

  1. On gaits they saw, the two teachers tie. Sole 0.804, joints and IMU 0.805, macro-F1 per step over six floor classes.
  2. On a gait they never saw, only the sole still works. With SONIC held out: sole 0.399, joints and IMU 0.095. The other two hold-outs point the same way.
  3. The joints and IMU teacher does worse than guessing on the new gait. It calls almost every SONIC step "edge". The independent re-check notes that this is below a random guesser (about 0.17), so "chance" undersells the failure.
  4. Coarse sole features survive new sensor parameters, not a new sole. Shifted parameters cost 2 points on seen gaits; a differently built sole or a foot-outline mask costs 19-22 points.
  5. For support state the sole is clearly better. Sole 0.719, joints and IMU 0.445 on seen gaits.

What was done

  • The question. For one footstep (touchdown to lift-off, at most 0.6 s), how well does each teacher name the floor class?
  • Classes. Grippy hard floor (hard_grip), slippery hard floor (hard_slip), soft, rough, slope, and edge (planks and steps). Truth is the simulator's class 0.1 s after touchdown. Steps on hard_mid (an extra hard-floor type that exists only in the test data) and steps that land on a boundary between floors have no class: they are labelled but not scored.
  • Gaits. G113 and G80 are two ordinary locomotion policies that step with a flat foot. SONIC is human-like heel-to-toe walking. The main setting trains on G113 and G80 and keeps SONIC unseen.
  • The sole teacher reads only the coarse sole features of the stance foot: four pressure blocks, four shear vectors, centre of pressure, total force and contact area. Step 1 showed that the fine pressure map does not survive a change of sensor model; these coarse features do. The right foot is mirrored.
  • The joints and IMU teacher reads the joints and the IMU over the same step window.
  • Same network and recipe for both. The step-1 temporal network (a causal dilated CNN). Settings were chosen on validation steps of the seen gaits only: 18 settings × 2 seeds, then reused for every hold-out. Each model trains in 10-20 s on a GPU.
  • Data. 215,393 walking steps from the step-1 data (train 141,307, validation 21,220, test 52,866). Scores are macro-F1 per step on the test split, mean ± sd over 3 seeds.

Its shifted labels agree with its stored-sensor labels on 88 % of steps.

Support-state teachers

A second question for the same teachers: is the foot fully supported, partly supported (over a gap or a step edge), or not in contact? Truth is the simulator's support state 0.1 s after touchdown. None means no contact (force along the sole normal of 2 N or less). Partial means contact with fewer than 90 % of the sole's 24 probe points over supporting ground; tilt and bumps of 2 cm or less do not count. Full is the rest. Same steps, inputs and recipe, with a three-way output weighted 1 / 3 / 1.

Independent re-check

  • Every headline number re-computed and confirmed: seen gaits, SONIC, the other hold-outs, all sensor variants (from the saved predictions), and the support teachers.
  • The sole teacher really reads only the coarse features. All 44 sole models take exactly the 16 coarse features plus a validity flag as input. Holds with a caveat: for both teachers the step window ends at the simulator's noise-free contact flag, not at a sensed one.
  • No leaks. The held-out gait is absent from the training and validation lists of every teacher, and no teacher list contains a test episode. When teachers label training steps for the vision head, they are cross-fitted: no teacher labels an episode it trained on (0 violations among 54,010 rows).
  • Not verified by the re-check: how the 288 separate test simulations and the pad-sole simulation were built. It only re-scored their saved predictions.

8

Limits

  • Simulation. The sole is a simulated sensor on a rigid box foot.
  • Three gaits. The unseen-gait result rests on three hold-outs, and the vision experiments use only one of them (SONIC).
  • Labels with limits. An "edge" step often never touches an edge, and the step-1 contact flag misses a foot loaded on its front edge (see the dataset page).
  • A sole built differently costs 19-22 points. A real sole will differ from the simulated one in more than its parameters.
